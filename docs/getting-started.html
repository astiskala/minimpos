Getting started

From an Adyen account to your first payment on the terminal. Most of it is done once, in your Adyen Customer Area and in Mini mPOS's Settings. Allow about half an hour, plus the time Adyen takes to deploy the app.

1. Before you start

  • An Adyen account with in-person payments (Terminal API), and a user for the Customer Area that can see device settings, upload apps and manage payments.
  • Adyen Android terminals running Android 9 or later, for example the S1F2, S1F4Pro, S1E4Pro, S1E2L, AMS1, S1U2 or SFO1. Printing needs a model with a printer; scanning receipts, barcodes and catalog codes needs a camera.
  • Optional: an SMTP mail server (host, port, user and password) if you want to email receipts.

Mini mPOS is an independent project, not an Adyen product. Try everything on TEST terminals before you take live payments.

2. Try it without a terminal

On an Android emulator or phone, Mini mPOS takes payments with its built-in simulator, so you can explore every screen first. Install it from the source code:

git clone https://github.com/astiskala/minimpos.git
cd minimpos
./gradlew :app:installDebug

Under Settings › Simulator you choose how simulated payments end: approved, declined, canceled, timed out or with a busy terminal.

3. Create a shared key

Mini mPOS encrypts every request to the terminal with a shared key, which you create in the Customer Area:

  1. Go to Devices › Device settings › Integrations › Terminal API › Encryption key, for the terminals (or the store or account) that will run Mini mPOS.
  2. Create the key, and note its key identifier, passphrase and version. Select the pencil icon to see them again later.

4. Put the app on your terminals

  1. Download minimpos-<version>.apk from the latest release. It is signed by the maintainer, which Adyen requires (an unsigned APK uploads, but the terminal refuses to install it).
  2. Upload it and deploy it to your terminals as described in Adyen's app deployment guide. Adyen converts the app for the terminals and installs it on the ones you choose.

Building and signing your own APK works too; see the README. Whichever you choose, stick to it: the Customer Area only accepts later versions signed with the same key.

5. Connect it to the terminal

  1. Open Mini mPOS on the terminal and tap Connect to this terminal on Home (or go to Settings › Terminal).
  2. Enter the key identifier and passphrase, and the version if it isn't 1.
  3. Tap Save and test (or the keyboard's Done key). The result is shown straight away.

The terminal's ID (POIID), its address and whether it is a TEST or LIVE terminal are detected, so there is nothing else to enter. Settings › About shows what was found, and a TEST banner reminds you when payments are not real.

To try a real terminal from a phone or emulator instead, choose Payments go to › A terminal on your network and also enter the terminal's IP address and POIID (as on its Device info screen).

6. Set up your business

Everything below is in Settings.

Payments
The currency (any of the 138 Adyen processes; automatic follows the device's country), whether checkout asks for a transaction reference and a reference prefix, saving cards (on by default or not, for sales and for pre-authorizations, the recurring model and whether the shopper reference comes from a customer reference or the email) and when to ask for the shopper's email.
Tax
Your named tax rates (such as GST 10% and GST-free 0%), whether prices include tax, the default rate for new products, and a Charge tax switch.
Receipts
Business name, address, tax ID, phone, title and footer; what the receipt shows (tax breakdown, references, refund QR code); printing automatically and the merchant copy.
Email (SMTP)
Your mail server, sender, an optional blind copy and the subject, with a test email.
Security
An admin PIN for Settings and Products, and how soon they lock again. Set one before the terminal goes on the counter.
Data
How long sales and refunds are kept, and clearing the history.

7. Add your products

In Products, tap + and enter a name, price and tax rate, and optionally a category and a barcode (type it or scan it). Categories, on the second tab, group the products on the sale screen.

A product can also be a pre-authorization product, such as a deposit or a hire bond; see Pre-authorizations.

To copy the catalog to another terminal, open the menu in Products and choose Share catalog (QR); on the other terminal choose Receive catalog (QR) and point its camera at the codes. Run the same version of Mini mPOS on both terminals.

8. Sell and refund

  • New sale: tap products, scan a barcode or add a custom amount, then Charge. At checkout, add a reference and (with the shopper's consent) save the card, then Pay and follow the terminal.
  • Receipts print as one slip with your header, the items, tax, the card receipt and a refund QR code, and can be emailed.
  • Refund: scan the QR code on the receipt, or open the sale in History. Refund everything, some items, or an amount. Adyen processes refunds asynchronously, so the app shows them as requested; the final outcome is in your Customer Area.
  • History groups transactions by day with daily totals. Needs attention lists payments whose result was not confirmed, which you can check again with the terminal.

9. Pre-authorizations

A pre-authorization holds an amount on the shopper's card without charging it, for example a deposit for an event, a room or equipment hire. You capture it later (the amount actually owed, up to the amount held), or cancel it to release the hold.

With Adyen, once

  • Ask Adyen Support to set up pre-authorization for your payment methods. Whether a card allows it depends on the card scheme and your business's merchant category code (MCC); see Adyen's pre-authorization guide.
  • You do not need to switch your account to manual capture: Mini mPOS asks for manual capture on each pre-authorization only (authorisationType=PreAuth with manualCapture=true), so normal sales are still captured as your account is set up.
  • Optional: smart pre-authorization (Customer Area, Devices › Device settings › Payment features) lets the terminal ask for another card when the one presented does not support it.

In Mini mPOS

  1. In Products, edit (or add) the product and switch on Pre-authorization product. It then no longer appears in New sale.
  2. Home now shows Pre-authorize beside New sale. A pre-authorization holds a single item, so tapping a product (or adding a custom amount, or scanning a barcode) goes straight to checkout; tap Pre-authorize there.
  3. At checkout, saving the card is on by default (Settings › Payments › Saving cards), so late costs can be charged once the pre-authorization has been captured. The receipt is titled PRE-AUTHORIZATION and shows the amount held.

Capture or cancel

  • Capture in the Customer Area: Payments › Payment list, select the PSP reference (shown in the app's History), then Capture payment from the payment actions. You can capture less than the amount held; the rest is released. Captures there do not show in the app.
  • Cancel in the app: open the pre-authorization in History and tap Cancel pre-authorization. If it has already been captured, Adyen refunds it in full instead.
  • Adjusting the amount held (up or down) is only possible with Adyen's Checkout API (/payments/{pspReference}/amountUpdates), not in the app or the Customer Area. If you need more than the hold, capture it and take the difference as a new sale.

Holds expire, after as little as a few days for some card schemes, and the shopper sees the amount as pending on their statement until then. Capture or cancel promptly; History's Pre-auths filter lists them, and the day totals show what is still held.

10. Before you go live

  • Run a few sales, refunds and (if you use them) pre-authorizations on a TEST terminal with Adyen's test cards.
  • Check the receipt layout with Settings › Receipts › Print test receipt.
  • Set an admin PIN, then deploy the same version to your LIVE terminals and enter their shared key.

11. If something goes wrong

Home says “Payments cannot reach the terminal”
Check the key identifier, passphrase and version in Settings › Terminal against the Customer Area, and tap Save and test.
The terminal refuses to install the app
An error such as INSTALL_PARSE_FAILED_NO_CERTIFICATES means the APK was not signed. Upload the signed minimpos-<version>.apk from the releases page.
A payment's result is unknown
Open it from History (Needs attention) and tap Check result again. Check the Customer Area before charging the shopper again.
“Unsupported catalog version”
The other terminal runs a newer Mini mPOS. Update both to the same version.

Still stuck? Open an issue on GitHub.
